Prepare Your Home for Storm Season in Fort Walton Beach, FL

Most storm water damage starts at a small weak spot. This guide walks through the checks that take an afternoon and the steps to take before a storm arrives.

Call (844) 950-6900

By the Fort Walton Beach Water Damage Pros team

Storm preparation is mostly about water paths. Rain finds the gaps in your roof, windows, doors and drainage. A few hours of checking each spring or early summer reduces the chance of an expensive loss. This guide is for homeowners in Fort Walton Beach and nearby towns.

Check the roof and the attic

  • Look for missing, lifted or cracked shingles from the ground.
  • Check flashing around chimneys, vents and skylights.
  • Look in the attic with a flashlight for stains or daylight where there should be none.
  • Clear debris from roof valleys.
  • Ask a roofer to inspect an older roof before storm season.
  • Never walk on a wet roof.

Windows, doors and the building envelope

Wind-driven rain pushes water through gaps that stay dry in ordinary weather. Check caulk and weatherstripping around windows and doors. Look for stains under sills. Replace worn seals. Garage doors and sliding doors are common entry points.

Drainage: gutters, downspouts and grading

  • Clean gutters and make sure downspouts discharge away from the foundation.
  • Check that the soil slopes away from the house.
  • Clear storm drains and swales near your property if allowed.
  • Know where water flows during heavy rain on your lot.
  • Keep sandbags or barriers if you live in a low area.

Your plan on the day

Know your evacuation zone and route. Keep a list of numbers, including your insurer. Charge phones. Take photos of the house before the storm. After the storm, do not enter standing water that may touch electrical items, and do not wade through floodwater. Cleanup after a storm is covered on flood damage cleanup and ceiling water damage repair.

Build a simple go-kit and paper file

  • Copies of your insurance policies, with the agent's phone number.
  • A list of valuables with photos.
  • Phone chargers, a battery pack and a flashlight.
  • Basic first aid supplies and any medications.
  • Cash in small bills.
  • A plastic bag or box for important papers.

Keep the kit where you can reach it quickly. Put paper copies in a waterproof bag, and keep digital copies in cloud storage.

After the storm: first safe steps

  • Wait for official clearance before returning home.
  • Stay away from downed power lines.
  • Do not use a generator indoors or in a garage.
  • Photograph the damage before cleanup.
  • Call your insurer and a water removal crew.
  • Keep receipts for temporary repairs and lodging.

Protecting the inside of the house

Water that gets past the roof often reaches electronics and papers first. Raise computers, files and keepsakes off the floor. Put valuable papers in a sealed bag. Move furniture away from exterior walls and windows. If you must leave, unplug small appliances and turn off the water at the main to reduce the chance of a burst line.

Know your water shutoffs and your power

Before a storm, find the water main and the electric panel. Label the breakers. If you evacuate, turn off the water at the main to limit damage from a burst pipe. Do not wade into flooded rooms to reach the panel. If the panel is in a flood-prone area, ask an electrician about elevating or relocating it before the next season.

Local note: what the Gulf can do to US 98

The National Weather Service reported that Hurricane Opal's surge destroyed US 98 between Fort Walton Beach and Destin when it came ashore on October 4, 1995.

The lesson is practical. Roads you rely on can close, and water can arrive from the Gulf side as well as from rain. Look up your address on the FEMA flood map, and ask the City of Fort Walton Beach floodplain administrator in the Planning and Zoning office at 105 Miracle Strip Parkway SW about your flood zone and elevation records.

If you live in Shalimar or Valparaiso, the county and city offices there answer permit and zoning questions.

Questions readers ask

When should I prepare my house for storm season?

Before the season starts. Roof and drainage checks are easiest in dry weather.

Does homeowners insurance cover storm water damage?

Wind damage and flood damage are often handled differently. Ask your insurer what your policy covers.

Should I buy flood insurance?

Standard policies usually exclude flood. Ask an insurance agent about flood coverage and the waiting period before it starts.

What should I photograph?

Each room, your belongings, the roof and exterior, and serial numbers of valuables.

Where can I find my flood zone in Fort Walton Beach?

Use the FEMA flood map service for your address, or ask the City of Fort Walton Beach floodplain administrator in Planning and Zoning.

Should I shut off the water before a storm?

If you evacuate, yes. It reduces the chance of a burst line flooding an empty house.

Are sandbags worth it?

They help redirect shallow water around doors and low spots. They do not stop deep flooding.
