Water Damage Categories 1, 2 and 3 in Fort Walton Beach, FL

Category 1, 2 and 3 describe how contaminated the water is. The number decides what can be saved and how the cleanup has to be done.

Call (844) 950-6900

By the Fort Walton Beach Water Damage Pros team

When you call about water damage, one of the first questions is where the water came from. The answer sets the category, and the category sets the plan. The categories come from the ANSI/IICRC S500 standard. They are not about how bad the damage looks. They are about how clean the water was.

The three categories

CategoryDescriptionCommon sources
1Clean water that does not pose a substantial risk.Broken supply line, faucet overflow, rain from a roof leak.
2Water with significant contamination that could cause discomfort.Washing machine, dishwasher, toilet overflow with urine only.
3Grossly contaminated waterSewage, floodwater from outside, standing water with growth.

Water can move to a higher category if it sits or touches contaminated surfaces.

Why water gets worse over time

Clean water does not stay clean in a building. It picks up dust, soil and material residue. Bacteria grow in warm, wet conditions. A Category 1 leak can become Category 2 after a day or two, and Category 2 can become Category 3. This is why fast action keeps the job simpler.

What each category means for your belongings

Category 1

Carpet, pad and drywall can often be dried in place if the work starts quickly.

Category 2

Porous materials are evaluated. Carpet may be saved, pad often is not.

Category 3

Porous materials such as carpet, pad and low drywall are usually removed. Hard surfaces are cleaned and sanitized.

What to do and what to avoid

  • Do not assume water is clean because it looks clear.
  • Do not touch Category 2 or 3 water with bare skin.
  • Do not run fans that blow contaminated water droplets around.
  • Photograph the source and the damage.
  • Do tell the dispatcher what you know about the source.
  • Do not try to sanitize sewage on your own.

Classes are different from categories

The S500 standard also uses Classes 1 to 4. Class describes how much water the materials absorbed and how fast they release it. A Class 1 loss affects a small area. Class 4 involves deeply saturated, hard-to-dry materials such as hardwood or concrete. A single job has both a category and a class. See structural drying for how class changes the equipment.

How the category changes the price

  • Category 3 jobs need protective gear, disposal and sanitizing.
  • More materials are removed, so rebuild scope grows.
  • Category 1 jobs often dry in place with less demolition.
  • Time since the loss can raise the category.
  • Your estimate is explained before work starts.

For floods and sewer problems, see flood damage cleanup and sewage backup cleanup.

Three short examples

A washing machine hose bursts

The water began as clean supply water but it crossed laundry detergent and floor soils. It is often handled as gray water if it sat.

A toilet overflows with waste

This is contaminated. Treat it as Category 3 and keep people out.

A river or storm surge enters the house

Water from outside carries soil and debris. It is Category 3.

Why you should not guess

Guessing the category can lead to saving materials that should go, or throwing out materials that could be saved. The crew is trained to evaluate. If you describe the source clearly on the phone, the dispatcher can usually tell which category to prepare for. Then the crew confirms on site.

Questions to ask about the water type

  • Did the water come from a supply line or a drain?
  • Did it touch a toilet, a sewer or the outdoors?
  • How long has it been wet?
  • Is there a smell, color change or slime?
  • Did it pass through insulation, soil or debris?

Share those answers with the dispatcher. They help the crew plan protective gear, disposal and what stays or goes.

What the categories do not tell you

A category does not tell you how big the job is. A small Category 3 spill can take less time than a large Category 1 flood. It also does not tell you what caused the water. The category is a way to decide how to protect people and which materials to keep. The scope still depends on area, materials, time and class.

Local note: sewage and the city system

The City of Fort Walton Beach Public Works and Utility Services department provides sewer service inside the city. If several homes on your street back up at the same time, the cause may be in the public system. Call the utility so it can check the main. Your private line is your responsibility, and a plumber clears it. Okaloosa County Water and Sewer serves other parts of the county.

Residents of Mary Esther and Shalimar should confirm which provider serves their address.

Questions readers ask

What is Category 3 water?

Grossly contaminated water such as sewage or floodwater. It requires protective gear and removal of porous materials.

Can Category 1 water become Category 3?

It can degrade over time if it sits or touches contaminated surfaces.

Is rain through the roof Category 1?

Often, but it can degrade as it passes through insulation and other materials.

Does the category affect insurance?

It can affect the scope of work. Ask your insurer how your policy treats each type.

Who decides the category in Fort Walton Beach?

The restoration crew evaluates the source and conditions on site, following the S500 standard.

Is gray water dangerous?

It can contain contaminants, so avoid contact and let the crew decide how to treat it.

Can I treat Category 3 water myself?

It is not recommended. Protective gear, containment and disposal are part of the job.
