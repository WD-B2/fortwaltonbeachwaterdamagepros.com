Check for Water Damage Before Buying in Fort Walton Beach, FL

Hidden water damage can cost more than the repair itself. Use this checklist on a showing, and know which records to request before you close.

Call (844) 950-6900

By the Fort Walton Beach Water Damage Pros team

A fresh coat of paint can hide a lot. When you are buying a house, you are looking for signs of past or current water entry. Some clues are visible, and some need a meter or an inspector. This guide covers what to look for on a walkthrough and what to ask afterward.

Walk the house with a flashlight

  • Ceilings: look for brown rings, fresh patches and mismatched texture.
  • Walls: look for bubbling paint, stains below windows and baseboards that gap.
  • Floors: press on tile and laminate for soft spots. Look for cupping in wood.
  • Cabinets: open the doors under sinks and look for stains, swelling and rust.
  • Windows and doors: check sills and frames for staining and soft wood.
  • Smell: a musty odor in a closed room is a clue.

Outside the house

  • Check the roof edges, gutters and downspouts.
  • See where water flows when it rains. Look for pooling near the foundation.
  • Look for staining or efflorescence, a white powder, on masonry.
  • Check the water heater, air handler and any attic equipment for rust or drain pan stains.
  • Look at the lot grading relative to the house.

Questions to ask the seller

  • Has there been any water damage, leak or flooding?
  • Were any insurance claims filed? Ask for the claim history if available.
  • Who did repairs, and are there invoices?
  • How old are the roof, water heater and supply lines?
  • Has the home been vacant or closed up for long?

A seller who is open about past repairs is usually easier to trust. Ask for receipts. Water damage that was dried properly is different from damage that was covered up. If you find active moisture after you buy, see structural drying and ceiling water damage repair.

A moisture-focused walkthrough order

  1. Start outside

    Walk the lot and note drainage, gutters and the condition of the roof edge.

  2. Go to the attic if you can

    Look for stains on the roof deck and insulation that looks compressed or discolored.

  3. Check wet rooms

    Kitchens, baths, laundry and the water heater area show most plumbing issues.

  4. Check the air handler

    Look at the drain pan and the ceiling beneath it.

  5. Finish with smells and sounds

    Close the house for a minute. A musty smell or a hissing sound is a clue.

What to do if you find signs

Do not panic, and do not assume the worst. Ask for repair records, request a moisture scan and ask for a credit or a repair before closing if the problem is real. A restoration professional can tell you whether the area is wet now or only stained. See wall and drywall water damage repair for what repairs involve.

Reading a home inspection report for moisture

A general inspection report may mention stains, past repairs or recommend further evaluation. Look for phrases such as evidence of past leak, elevated moisture reading or recommend plumber. Ask the inspector what the readings were and where. If a defect is listed, get written quotes before the contingency period ends so you can negotiate with real numbers.

Costs to keep in mind before you make an offer

Water damage can hide behind a low price. Ask for a credit if repairs are needed. Ask a contractor for a written estimate for the items an inspector flags. Remember that insurance may not cover prior damage. A buyer who asks for records, scans and quotes has much better footing in negotiation than one who relies on a seller's reassurance.

If a scan finds moisture, a restoration crew can dry it to the ANSI/IICRC S500 standard before you close, and the final readings give you a record to show your lender or insurer.

Local note: flood zone and elevation records

The City of Fort Walton Beach floodplain administrator sits in the Planning and Zoning office at 105 Miracle Strip Parkway SW. Ask whether an elevation certificate exists for the property. Elevation certificates have been required for buildings in special flood hazard areas since February 6, 1991. Also look up the property on the FEMA flood map. Your lender and insurer will ask about the flood zone, so get the answer before closing.

Buyers looking in Destin or Valparaiso should ask each city the same questions.

Questions readers ask

What are signs of past water damage?

Stains, patched drywall, mismatched paint, warped floors, rusty fixtures and a musty smell.

Should I order a moisture scan?

It is a useful add-on to a general inspection, especially if you see stains.

Can I ask for flood zone records?

Yes. Use the FEMA flood map and ask the local floodplain administrator.

Does a seller have to disclose water damage?

Disclosure rules are set by state law and your contract. Ask a real estate attorney.

Who do I ask about flood records in Fort Walton Beach?

The city floodplain administrator in Planning and Zoning.

Should I walk away if I find old water damage?

Not always. Old, dried and repaired damage is common. Ask for repair records and a moisture scan.

Can a restoration company inspect before I buy?

Many can scan for moisture. Ask the dispatcher whether a pre-purchase scan is possible for your timeline.
