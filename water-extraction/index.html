Emergency Water Extraction in Fort Walton Beach, FL

Standing water is soaking into your floors and walls right now. Call the dispatch line and a licensed, insured crew can be sent to pump, extract and begin drying.

Call (844) 950-6900
24/7 dispatch line open

Standing water keeps spreading into flooring, baseboards and furniture the longer it sits. Our dispatch line is open 24/7, and the call takes a couple of minutes. While you wait for the crew, stay out of the water if any cord, outlet or appliance touches it, and shut off the water source if you can do that safely.

What to do while you wait for the extraction crew

  • Stop the source. Close the fixture valve or the main shutoff if the water is still running.
  • Do not wade through water that reaches an outlet, power strip or appliance. Ask for help with the breaker if the panel is in a wet area.
  • Photograph the water line, the damaged items and the source before you move anything.
  • Lift small furniture, rugs, books and electronics onto dry surfaces such as counters or beds.
  • Keep children and pets out of the room. Do not use a household vacuum on water.
  • Treat water that came from a toilet, a drain or outside the house as contaminated and stay out of it.
  • If you smell gas, see sparks or hear buzzing near wet wiring, leave and call 911 or the utility before you call us.

How emergency water extraction works, step by step

  1. Triage on the phone

    The dispatcher asks where the water came from, how deep it is, which rooms are wet and whether the power is off. Those answers decide what equipment goes on the truck.

  2. Bulk water comes out first

    Crews pump deep water with submersible pumps. Truck-mounted or portable extractors then pull water out of carpet, padding and hard floors.

  3. Meters show what stayed wet

    A moisture meter and a thermal camera find water inside walls, under flooring and behind cabinets. Surface checks alone miss most of it.

  4. Drying starts the same visit

    Air movers and dehumidifiers go in so moisture stops climbing into drywall and framing. The plan follows the ANSI/IICRC S500 water damage standard.

Crews often combine two or three of these in one visit.

Extraction removes the water. Drying removes the moisture.

Pulling standing water out is only the first half of the job. Wood, drywall and padding hold moisture long after the surface looks dry. That moisture is what turns into warped floors and musty odor. Plan on structural drying with air movers and dehumidifiers right after extraction, and ask the crew to show you meter readings before and after.

What changes the scope and the price of extraction

  • Water category: clean supply water, gray water from appliances, or contaminated water from a drain or flood.
  • Depth and square footage of the wet area, and how many rooms or floors it reached.
  • Flooring type. Carpet with padding, tile, laminate and hardwood each need a different method.
  • Time since the loss. Water that sat overnight has usually reached the walls.
  • Access and logistics, such as stairs, a crawl space or limited parking.
  • Whether drying, demolition of wet materials or sanitizing has to follow.

We explain the scope and price before work starts, after the crew has looked at what the meter finds. Pricing by phone would be a guess.

Water extraction in Fort Walton Beach homes

Many Gulf Coast houses sit on concrete slabs. Water spills out across the slab and wicks into drywall and baseboards at the edges of every room. Warm, humid air then slows evaporation, so materials stay wet longer than you would expect. That is why a room that looks fine after a mop can still have wet walls two days later.

If the water came from a pipe, see burst pipe water damage. If a storm pushed it in, storm water damage cleanup covers the added steps.

Insurance and water extraction

Sudden and accidental water damage is often covered by homeowners policies, while slow leaks and neglected maintenance often are not. Flood damage usually needs separate flood coverage. Ask your insurer what your policy says, and keep your photos and the written scope from the crew.

Questions we hear from Fort Walton Beach callers

Do you handle water extraction emergencies at night or on weekends in Fort Walton Beach?

Yes. The dispatch line runs 24/7, including nights, weekends and holidays. Call, describe the water and follow the safety steps while the crew is arranged. Response depends on crew availability, and the dispatcher tells you what to expect when you call.

How long does water extraction take?

A single room often takes a few hours, and a whole floor takes longer. Depth, floor type and access change the time. Drying usually continues for days after the standing water is gone.

Can I use a shop vacuum to remove standing water myself?

A wet-rated shop vacuum helps with a small spill. It cannot handle a flooded room, and it does not reach water in padding or walls. Never use an ordinary household vacuum on water.

Is it safe to turn the power off myself?

Only if the breaker panel is dry and you can reach it without stepping in water. If not, stay out and tell the dispatcher so the crew can handle power safely.

Do I need extraction if the floor only looks damp?

Often yes. A damp surface can hide wet padding, subfloor or wall bottoms. A moisture meter check tells you whether extraction or only drying is needed.
