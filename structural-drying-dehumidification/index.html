Structural Drying and Dehumidification in Fort Walton Beach, FL

Walls can stay wet for days after the water is gone. Structural drying uses airflow, dehumidifiers and daily meter readings to get materials dry.

Why do my walls and floors still feel damp days after the leak?

Mopping or vacuuming removes the surface water, but drywall, framing, subfloor and padding hold moisture inside. In humid coastal air, that moisture leaves slowly. A damp smell, a cold wall, swollen baseboards and floors that cup or lift are all signs. Call the dispatch line, which is open 24/7, and ask for a moisture check before you close up the walls or repaint.

What water mitigation means for your home

Water mitigation is the work done to stop damage from getting worse. It includes finding the source, removing standing water, removing materials that cannot be saved and drying everything else to a measured standard. Structural drying is the longest part of that process, and it is where most of the cost and most of the risk of mold sits.

How structural drying works

Air movers

These fans push dry air across wet surfaces and into wall cavities so moisture leaves materials as vapor.

Dehumidifiers

LGR or desiccant units pull moisture out of the air so drying continues instead of stalling.

Moisture meters

Pin and pinless meters read wood and drywall. Daily readings show whether drying is on track.

Thermal imaging

A thermal camera finds cool, wet areas behind walls and under floors. It guides where to place equipment.

The setup follows the ANSI/IICRC S500 water damage standard, which describes psychrometrics, drying goals and monitoring. Ask any company whether its crew works to that standard. Equipment is placed and monitored by the crew. We do not rent equipment for do-it-yourself setups.

A typical drying schedule

  1. Day one: set and measure

    The crew records starting readings, places equipment and sets drying goals based on a dry reference material in your home.

  2. Days two and three: check and adjust

    Readings are taken again. Equipment is moved, added or removed depending on progress.

  3. Later days: finish

    Drying ends when materials reach the goal. Equipment is removed and final readings are recorded for your file.

Class of loss: how much water the materials hold

Under the ANSI/IICRC S500 standard, a water loss is also given a class. Class 1 affects a small area with low-absorbency materials. Class 2 affects a whole room with wet carpet and wall bottoms. Class 3 involves water that came from above and soaked ceilings, walls and insulation. Class 4 involves specialty drying, such as hardwood, plaster or concrete that holds water deeply.

The class sets how much equipment is used and where it goes. A Class 3 loss from a ceiling leak needs airflow high in the room. A Class 4 loss may need heat, mats and weeks of monitoring.

Equipment you will see in your home

LGR dehumidifier

A low-grain refrigerant unit that removes a lot of water from warm, humid air.

Desiccant dehumidifier

Uses a drying wheel and works in cooler or very damp conditions.

Axial air mover

A high-volume fan for open areas and floors.

Centrifugal air mover

A directed blower for wall cavities and under cabinets.

Floor drying mats

Pull moisture from wood and subfloor under controlled conditions.

Air scrubber

Filters air when dust or contaminants are present.

What affects the cost of structural drying

  • Number of wet rooms and square footage of materials.
  • Material types and thickness.
  • Water category and class of the loss.
  • Indoor humidity, which is high on much of the Gulf Coast.
  • Number of drying days and number of equipment pieces.
  • Whether demolition or sanitizing is needed first.

Humidity and drying in Fort Walton Beach

Warm, humid air holds a lot of moisture, so open windows can slow drying instead of helping it. Dehumidifiers and a closed house work better. After a pipe break, see burst pipe cleanup. After a flood, see flood damage cleanup.

Insurance note for drying

Many insurers want drying records, such as dated meter readings and equipment logs. Ask your insurer what they need, and keep the daily log your crew provides.

Questions we hear from Fort Walton Beach callers

Why do walls still feel damp days after the leak?

Moisture sits inside drywall, framing and subfloor and leaves slowly. Without airflow and dehumidification, it can linger and support mold growth.

How long does structural drying take?

Often three to five days, but it depends on materials, water category, humidity and equipment. Meter readings decide when it is done.

Do you handle drying emergencies at night or on weekends in Fort Walton Beach?

The dispatch line runs 24/7, so you can call any time. Drying itself is a multi-day process, and the crew schedules equipment visits around your needs.

Can I just run fans and open windows?

Household fans and open windows rarely dry a wet wall cavity, and humid outdoor air can make it worse. Controlled drying with dehumidifiers and meters is more reliable.

Is mold removal part of drying?

Drying prevents conditions that support mold. If mold is already present, ask a mold professional. ANSI/IICRC S520 covers mold remediation.
