Warm floor, high water bill or damp carpet? Signs of a slab leak

A slab leak is a break in a water line that runs under or inside the concrete foundation. You rarely see the pipe, so you notice the side effects. Our dispatch line is open 24/7, and the first call helps you decide what to do while you confirm the source.

  • A floor that feels warm in one spot. A leaking hot line heats the concrete above it.
  • A water bill that climbs with no change in use.
  • The sound of running water when every tap and appliance is off.
  • Damp carpet, lifted tile, or baseboards that swell for no clear reason.
  • Cracks that appear in tile or along walls, and a musty smell at the floor level.
  • A water meter that keeps moving when everything is off.
  • If you smell gas, see sparks or hear buzzing near wet wiring, leave and call 911 or the utility before you call us.

How to test before you call

Turn off all water use in the house and check the meter. If the dial or low-flow indicator moves, water is escaping somewhere. Close the main valve at the house and check again. If the meter still moves, the leak is on the line between the meter and the house. If it stops, the leak is inside. A licensed plumber confirms with leak detection equipment.

How a slab leak gets dried

  1. Isolate the leak

    The plumber shuts off the affected line so water stops reaching the house.

  2. Map moisture

    Meters and thermal imaging show how far water has traveled under flooring, through baseboards and into drywall.

  3. Remove wet materials

    Carpet, padding, laminate or swollen baseboard that cannot dry are removed. Concrete itself dries slowly and is monitored.

  4. Dry to a documented goal

    Air movers and dehumidifiers run, often with floor drying mats or injection drying. The plan follows ANSI/IICRC S500. See structural drying for details.

What leak detection looks like

A plumber uses acoustic listening devices, thermal imaging and pressure testing to find a slab leak without breaking concrete everywhere. Once the line is located, the repair may be made through the slab, by rerouting the line through walls or attic, or by replacing the line entirely. Which option fits depends on the age and layout of your plumbing. Get the plumber's findings in writing, because your insurer may ask for them.

What the cost of slab leak damage depends on

  • How long the leak ran before it was found.
  • Which rooms and how many square feet of flooring got wet.
  • Floor covering. Tile can be dried in place, carpet and laminate usually cannot.
  • Whether the leak was hot or cold supply water.
  • Drying days needed for concrete and framing.
  • Plumbing repair and rebuild costs, which are separate from restoration.

Why slab leaks are hard to notice in coastal Florida

Many Gulf Coast homes are slab-on-grade, so supply lines run under the floor with no basement or crawl space to inspect. Humid air also hides the smell of moisture. By the time floors feel soft, water has often been moving through the slab for a while. Combine meter checks with the other clues. If you see ceiling or wall stains instead, read about ceiling water damage or burst pipe damage.

Insurance note for slab leaks

Coverage for slab leaks varies widely. Some policies cover sudden discovery and resulting damage, and many exclude the pipe itself and gradual leaks. Ask your insurer before work begins, and keep the plumber's detection report.

Questions we hear from Fort Walton Beach callers

How do I know if I have a slab leak?

Common signs are a warm floor spot, a rising water bill, running water sounds with taps off, damp carpet and a water meter that moves when all use stops. A plumber confirms with detection equipment.

Do you handle slab leak emergencies at night or on weekends in Fort Walton Beach?

Yes. The 24/7 line is open at all hours. If water is surfacing or soaking carpet, call right away. The plumber locates the leak, and the restoration crew removes water and dries.

Can concrete be dried?

Yes, slowly. Concrete holds moisture for a long time, so crews monitor readings over days and use dehumidifiers and mats where needed.

Do you fix the broken pipe?

No. A licensed plumber repairs or reroutes the pipe. We extract water and dry the structure afterward.

Should I move out during a slab leak?

Not always. Stay out of rooms with standing water near electrical outlets. Otherwise the plumber and crew can advise based on what they find.
