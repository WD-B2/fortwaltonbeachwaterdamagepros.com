Document Water Damage for Insurance in Fort Walton Beach, FL

A clear file makes a claim easier to explain. Here is what to capture in the first hour, during cleanup and afterward.

Call (844) 950-6900

By the Fort Walton Beach Water Damage Pros team

An adjuster cannot see what happened on the day water came in. Your photos, notes and logs tell the story. A good file shortens back-and-forth and avoids disputes about what was damaged. This guide shows what to collect. It is general information, not legal or insurance advice.

The first hour: capture before you clean

  • Photograph and video every wet room from several angles.
  • Capture the source, such as the broken pipe or failed hose.
  • Photograph the waterline on walls and furniture.
  • Record date and time.
  • Photograph serial numbers on damaged appliances and electronics.
  • Keep the failed part in a bag.

A simple file structure

FolderWhat goes in it
Photos and videoBefore, during and after cleanup.
Source reportPlumber, roofer or HVAC invoice and notes.
Drying logDaily meter readings and equipment list.
Contents listItem, age, cost, photo.
ReceiptsCleanup, lodging, repairs.
Insurer communicationsDates, names, claim numbers.

Keep digital copies in two places.

Make a contents list

List damaged items by room. Include a description, approximate age, cost and photo. Do not throw things away before the adjuster has seen them, unless they are unsafe or contaminated. If you must discard, photograph first and keep a sample, such as a piece of carpet.

During cleanup: keep records of what is removed

  • Ask the crew for a written scope and daily readings.
  • Photograph materials that are cut out.
  • Note the date each piece of equipment is placed and removed.
  • Keep invoices for every service.
  • Write down every conversation with the insurer.

After the cleanup

Keep the final dry readings. They show the structure was dried to standard. Keep the rebuild estimates and invoices. If you find more damage later, add it to the file with dated photos. Read structural drying and water extraction to see what the crew's records should show.

What not to do

Do not exaggerate or guess at values. Do not discard damaged items without photos. Do not sign documents you do not understand. Do not delay calling your insurer.

How to photograph a loss well

  • Take wide shots of each room, then close-ups of the damage.
  • Include a tape measure or common object for scale.
  • Photograph the waterline at the same height in several rooms.
  • Record short video walkthroughs while narrating what you see.
  • Photograph labels and serial numbers.
  • Back up photos the same day.

Common documentation mistakes

MistakeWhy it hurtsFix
Cleaning first, photographing laterNo proof of the original condition.Photograph before moving anything.
No datesHard to show timeline.Keep timestamps and a written log.
Vague contents listSlows the claimList item, age, cost and photo.
Verbal-only conversationsDisputes laterFollow phone calls with an email.

A simple, dated file beats a perfect but late one.

Working with the adjuster

Adjusters review your file and may visit the property. Be present if you can. Walk them through each room and point out the source and the waterline. Ask what they need next and when to expect a response. Keep your own notes of the visit. If you disagree with the findings, ask for the reasoning in writing.

Update the file the same day you receive new documents.

A file kept in date order saves time when the adjuster asks a question months later.

Local note: supplemental claims

Florida Statute 627.70132 allows a supplemental claim, which covers more damage found after the first claim, if notice is given within 18 months of the date of loss. A supplemental claim is easier to support when the first file shows dry readings and a clear scope. Check your own policy for additional rules.

Policyholders in Mary Esther and Destin follow the same Florida rules.

Questions readers ask

What should I photograph first?

The source, the standing water and every wet room before anything is moved.

Should I keep damaged items?

Keep them until the adjuster approves disposal, unless they are unsafe.

Will the restoration company document for me?

Yes. Photos, readings and a written scope are standard. We do not promise claim outcomes.

Do I need receipts?

Yes. Keep every one.

How long can I add damage to a claim in Florida?

Statute 627.70132 sets 18 months for a supplemental claim. Check your policy.

Should I wait for the adjuster before cleaning up?

Do not delay mitigation. Photograph first, then start. Ask the insurer what they need.

What if I lost receipts?

Ask vendors for copies. Bank statements can help reconstruct payments.
