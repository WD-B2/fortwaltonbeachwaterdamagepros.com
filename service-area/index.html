Who handles what in Okaloosa County

Several offices touch a water damage job. The restoration crew removes water and dries the structure. Your city or town building department answers permit questions. The City of Fort Walton Beach Public Works and Utility Services department provides water, sewer and stormwater service inside city limits. Okaloosa County Water and Sewer serves other areas. When a problem starts at the street, call the utility. When water is inside your building, call us.

  • Permits: ask the building or growth management office for your town.
  • Flood zones: look up your address on the FEMA flood map.
  • Utility outages and street leaks: call the utility that serves your address.
  • Claims: call your insurer, and keep photos and receipts.

Why the corridor matters

US 98 and Eglin Parkway are the two main routes between these towns. Storms and heavy rain can slow either one, so call early. A crew plans the route when you describe the address. Related pages cover storm water damage and flood damage cleanup.

Choosing the right page for your address

Each town page lists the ZIP codes, the direction and drive from Fort Walton Beach, the local offices to ask about permits and the utilities that serve the area. If you live between towns, use the page for the town on your mailing address. If you are unsure, call and give the dispatcher your street.

What the crew needs to know about your address

  • The street address and ZIP code.
  • Gate codes or building access rules for condos and communities.
  • Parking limits near the building.
  • Whether someone will be home to let the crew in.
  • Where the water source and main shutoff are, if you know.
  • Which floor the water is on, especially in multi-story buildings.

Condos and apartments often have association rules about contractors, work hours and elevators. Tell your property manager at the same time you call.

Storms, roads and timing

US 98 and Eglin Parkway carry most of the traffic between Fort Walton Beach and the nearby towns. Heavy rain, a storm or a crash on either road can slow a trip. Call as early as you can, and tell the dispatcher about any road closures you know of. After a serious storm, many properties need help at once, so honest expectations matter more than promises.

If you do not see your town, call anyway. The dispatcher can tell you whether the address is within reach, and the answer is better than a guess from a map.

Tell the dispatcher the cross streets or a nearby landmark if your address is new or hard to find on a map. Rural roads, new subdivisions and gated communities can confuse navigation, and a clear description saves a crew several minutes.

Mail ZIP codes versus city boundaries

A mailing address does not always match a city line. Some addresses use a Fort Walton Beach ZIP code but sit outside city limits, and some town addresses sit near the edge of another jurisdiction. That matters for permits and utilities, so ask your town or county office which one applies. For the dispatch line, the street address is what counts.

When to call before you worry about the address

  • Water is spreading and you are not sure your town is covered. Call anyway.
  • You smell gas or see sparks. Leave and call 911 or the utility first.
  • A sewage smell or backup. Do not touch the water and call.

Crews serving the area work to the ANSI/IICRC S500 water damage standard, whichever town the job is in.
