How to Shut Off Your Home Water Main in Fort Walton Beach, FL

The quickest way to limit water damage is to know where your main shutoff is before you need it. Here is how to find it, test it and use it.

Call (844) 950-6900

By the Fort Walton Beach Water Damage Pros team

When a pipe bursts, every minute of running water adds to the damage. A homeowner who can turn off the main in thirty seconds saves a lot of drywall. Most people do not know where theirs is. This guide shows where to look, how to test the valve and what to do if it will not turn.

Where to look for the main shutoff

Outside at the meter

Look for a rectangular lid in the ground near the street or sidewalk. The meter and a utility valve sit under it. The utility owns this valve.

On the house side

Many homes have a second valve where the line enters the house, often near the water heater, in a garage or on an exterior wall.

Inside a utility area

Check a laundry room, mechanical closet or garage wall. The valve is on the pipe that comes in from outside.

Condos and apartments

A unit may have its own shutoff under a sink or in a closet. Ask building management where the building main is.

Valve types and how to turn them

ValveHow to closeTip
Gate valve (round wheel)Turn clockwise until it stops.Turn slowly. Old gate valves can stick or break.
Ball valve (lever handle)Turn the handle a quarter turn so it sits across the pipe.Handle across the pipe means closed.
Meter valveUtility-owned. Use only in an emergency.Call the utility if the house valve fails.

Test your valve once a year so it does not seize.

What to do right now if water is running

  • Close the main shutoff valve.
  • Open a faucet at the lowest point to drain the lines.
  • Turn off the water heater if the tank might drain through the break.
  • Stay out of rooms where water touches outlets or ceiling lights.
  • Take photos of the break and the damage.
  • Call a plumber for the repair and the dispatch line for water removal.

When the valve will not turn

Do not force a stuck valve with a wrench. It may snap and make the leak worse. Close the utility valve at the meter if you can reach it, using a meter key or large pliers if your utility allows it. If you cannot, call the utility and tell them it is an emergency. They have tools to shut off service. For water already in the house, water extraction and structural drying come next.

Practice before you need it

  • Find the valve and label it with a tag.
  • Turn it off and on once a year.
  • Make sure everyone in the house knows where it is.
  • Keep a valve key or wrench nearby.
  • Consider a smart leak sensor with automatic shutoff.

What if you rent or live in a condo?

Renters should ask the landlord where the shutoff is the day they move in. Condo owners should ask the association or property manager. In multi-unit buildings, a main valve may serve several homes, and shutting it off can affect neighbors. Follow the building rules, and tell the manager right away when you use it.

After the water is off

  1. Drain the lines

    Open the lowest faucet and one upstairs to release water and pressure.

  2. Protect what is dry

    Move belongings off the floor and lift rugs.

  3. Call the plumber

    Ask whether the break is on your side of the meter.

  4. Call for water removal

    The crew extracts and dries. The burst pipe page shows what that looks like.

Signs the main valve needs replacing

  • The handle spins freely without stopping the flow.
  • Water drips from the stem when you turn it.
  • Corrosion or green buildup on the valve body.
  • The valve is stiff enough that you cannot turn it by hand.
  • A plumber recommends replacement during another visit.

Replacing an old gate valve with a quarter-turn ball valve makes emergencies much easier. It is a small job for a licensed plumber, and it costs far less than cleaning up behind a burst line.

Know your shutoffs beyond the main

Fixture valves under sinks and behind toilets can stop a local leak without cutting water to the whole house. Learn which ones turn easily. Many are the small oval handles marked with a quarter turn. Replace any that are stuck or leaking. Having both the main and the fixture valves in good order gives you two ways to stop the water.

Local note: which utility serves you?

Utility service differs by town. The City of Fort Walton Beach Public Works and Utility Services department provides water inside city limits. Mary Esther operates its own groundwater system, according to EPA records. Destin Water Users, Inc. serves Destin, and Okaloosa County Water and Sewer serves county areas. Know which one bills you, because that is the office to call about the meter valve.

If your leak is in Mary Esther or Destin, mention the town when you call so the right resources are used.

Questions readers ask

Where is the main water shutoff in most homes?

Often near the water heater, in a garage, on an exterior wall or near where the line enters the house. Many homes also have a utility valve at the meter.

Is it safe to use the street meter valve?

It belongs to the utility. Use it only in an emergency, and tell the utility afterward.

Do I need a plumber to shut off water?

No. Any homeowner can close the valve. A plumber is needed for the repair.

What if I have a condo?

Ask building management where the building main is. Some units have their own shutoff.

Which utility do I call in Fort Walton Beach?

In city limits, Public Works and Utility Services provides water. Outside the city, check your bill to find the utility.

Will shutting off the main damage anything?

No. Closing it slowly is safe. Turn off the water heater first if the tank will be drained.

How do I restart the water afterward?

Open the main slowly, open a faucet to let air out and check fittings for leaks. If a pipe was repaired, have the plumber confirm.
