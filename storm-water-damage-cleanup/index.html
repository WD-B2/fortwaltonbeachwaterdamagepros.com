24/7 dispatch line open

Storms push water into houses through roofs, windows, doors and low spots. Our dispatch line is open 24/7, including after storms when many neighbors are calling too. Stay safe first. Do not go outside during high winds, and do not walk through water that may touch downed power lines.

After the storm: what to do and what to avoid

  • Wait until the weather and any evacuation orders allow safe movement.
  • Do not touch downed lines or wade through water that may be energized.
  • Cover openings with tarps or boards only if you can do it safely from the ground or a stable surface.
  • Photograph roof damage, broken windows, wet rooms and the waterline before cleanup.
  • Do not run wet appliances or HVAC equipment until it is checked.
  • Call your insurer, then call the dispatch line to schedule water removal and drying.
  • If you smell gas, see sparks or hear buzzing near wet wiring, leave and call 911 or the utility before you call us.

How storm water gets into a Gulf Coast home

Wind lifts shingles and drives rain under flashing. Windows and doors leak at the sills when pressure builds. Surge and heavy rain flow in at ground level. Each path leaves a different pattern. Roof leaks stain ceilings and wet insulation. Ground-level water sits on floors and wicks into walls.

Why local storm history matters for your plan

Hurricane Opal came ashore in early October 1995, and the National Weather Service reported that its surge destroyed US 98 between Fort Walton Beach and Destin. That shows how quickly water can reach roads and low homes near the coast.

In September 2020, Hurricane Sally made landfall near Gulf Shores, Alabama, and waves still pounded docks near the Brooks Bridge here. The takeaway is simple. Know your flood zone on the FEMA map, know your policy and keep this number handy before the next storm.

Storm cleanup, step by step

  1. Make it safe

    The crew checks for electrical hazards, structural damage and unsafe areas before work begins.

  2. Extract and tarp

    Water is removed, wet debris is cleared and temporary protection stops further entry.

  3. Remove what cannot be dried

    Soaked insulation, drywall and carpet are removed. Floodwater is treated as contaminated. See flood damage cleanup.

  4. Dry and document

    Air movers and dehumidifiers dry the structure. Records support your claim. See structural drying.

Tarps, boards and temporary protection

When a storm opens the roof or breaks a window, temporary protection stops more water from coming in. A tarp or board keeps the next rain out until a roofer or glass contractor can repair the opening. Insurers generally expect you to take reasonable steps like this, and the receipts can be part of your claim. Do not climb onto a wet roof yourself.

What affects the cost of storm water cleanup

  • Water category and how it entered the home.
  • Rooms and floors reached.
  • Roof or window openings that need temporary protection.
  • Contents moved, cleaned or discarded.
  • Drying days and equipment count.
  • Rebuild work after drying.

Insurance note for storm losses

Wind damage and flood damage are often handled under different coverage. Your policy may have separate deductibles for named storms. Ask your insurer, keep photos and receipts and read your policy for notice deadlines.

Roof openings often lead to ceiling water damage. Lower-level flooding may follow the steps for basement and lower-level water damage.

Storm cleanup uses the same ANSI/IICRC S500 framework for water categories and classes, which is why the source of the water matters so much to the plan.

Questions we hear from Fort Walton Beach callers

Do you handle storm water damage emergencies at night or on weekends in Fort Walton Beach?

Yes. The dispatch line is open 24/7. After a major storm, many homes need help at once, so the dispatcher gives you an honest picture of crew availability.

Is storm water contaminated?

Water that enters at ground level or through drains is generally treated as Category 3. Rain that enters through a roof is usually cleaner but can degrade.

Do you repair roofs?

No. A roofer repairs the roof. We remove water and dry the structure.

When should I file a claim?

Contact your insurer as soon as possible. Florida law sets deadlines for notice of a claim, so ask your insurer about the timeline for your policy.

Can I stay in the house?

It depends on safety, power and how much of the house is wet. The crew and your insurer can help you decide.
