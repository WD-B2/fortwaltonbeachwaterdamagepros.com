Gurgling drains, toilet overflow or a sewage smell? What it means

Sewage in a house usually shows up as a toilet that will not flush, water rising in a tub or floor drain, gurgling sounds, or a foul odor. This is contaminated water. Our dispatch line is open 24/7, and the first thing to do is stop using sinks, tubs, toilets and appliances so no more waste enters the line.

  • Stop all water use, including flushing, showers, the dishwasher and the washing machine.
  • Keep people and pets out of the affected area.
  • Do not touch the water with bare skin. Wear gloves and boots if you must enter.
  • Do not use fans or vacuums that spread contaminated droplets through the house.
  • Turn off power to the area only if the panel is dry and you can reach it safely.
  • Call a plumber to clear the blockage and call us to remove the water and clean.
  • If you smell gas, see sparks or hear buzzing near wet wiring, leave and call 911 or the utility before you call us.

Why sewage water is handled differently

Under the IICRC S500 standard, water from sewage lines is Category 3. It can carry bacteria and other contaminants, so the work is done with protective gear and containment. Porous materials that soaked up the water, such as carpet, padding and drywall, are normally removed. Hard surfaces are cleaned and sanitized.

The sewage cleanup process

  1. Contain and protect

    Crew members put on protective equipment. Doors and vents are sealed to keep contamination from spreading to clean rooms.

  2. Extract and remove

    Contaminated water is pumped out. Carpet, padding and affected drywall are removed and bagged.

  3. Clean and sanitize

    Floors, framing and hard surfaces are cleaned and treated with antimicrobial products suited to the surface.

  4. Dry and verify

    Air movers, dehumidifiers and air filtration finish the job. See structural drying for how readings are checked.

What the crew wears and why it matters

Crews working in sewage wear gloves, boots, eye protection and respirators suited to the job. They keep clean and dirty zones separate and bag contaminated materials for proper disposal. If a crew arrives without protective gear for a sewage job, that is a warning sign. You can ask what protocol they follow.

What drives the cost of sewage backup cleanup

  • Volume of water and the number of rooms reached.
  • How much porous material must be removed.
  • Whether the backup came from inside or outside the house.
  • Cleaning, sanitizing and air filtration requirements.
  • Drying days and equipment needed.
  • Rebuild items such as drywall, trim and flooring.

Sewage backup after storms in Fort Walton Beach

Heavy rain can overwhelm drainage and push water back through low drains. If several homes on your street back up at the same time, the problem may be in the municipal system and not in your own line. The City of Fort Walton Beach Public Works and Utility Services department handles city sewer, so ask them whether a main-line issue exists. Related pages include storm water damage and flood damage cleanup.

Insurance note for sewage backup

Standard homeowners policies often exclude sewer backup unless you added a water backup endorsement. Ask your insurer whether you have one, and document the water level, damaged items and plumber report.

Questions we hear from Fort Walton Beach callers

Do you handle sewage backup emergencies at night or on weekends in Fort Walton Beach?

Yes. The dispatch line is open 24/7. Stop using water, stay out of the area and call so a crew can arrange safe removal and sanitizing.

What should I do first when sewage backs up?

Stop all water use, keep people and pets away from the water and call. A plumber clears the blockage and the restoration crew cleans up.

Can I clean up sewage myself?

It is not recommended. The water is contaminated, and porous materials need removal. Professionals use protective gear, containment and proper disposal.

Will carpet be saved after a sewage backup?

Usually not. Carpet and padding that touched Category 3 water are normally removed because they cannot be fully sanitized.

Is sewage backup covered by insurance?

Often only with a water backup endorsement. Ask your insurer and review your policy.
