Water Damage in a Rental Home in Fort Walton Beach, FL

When a rental floods or leaks, tenants and landlords both have jobs to do. This guide sorts out who does what in the first days.

Call (844) 950-6900

By the Fort Walton Beach Water Damage Pros team

Water damage in a rental raises two questions at once. How do we stop the water, and who is responsible for the cleanup? The answer depends on the lease, the cause and how fast each side acts. This guide gives a practical order of steps without legal advice.

Tenant steps in the first hour

  • Close the water source if you can do it safely.
  • Tell the landlord or property manager right away, by phone and in writing.
  • Photograph and video the damage and your belongings.
  • Move your valuables to dry areas.
  • Do not hire repairs on your own without checking the lease.
  • Call your renters insurance company if you have a policy.

Landlord steps

  • Confirm the water is off and the building is safe.
  • Call a plumber or the appropriate trade for the cause.
  • Arrange water removal and drying quickly.
  • Notify the property insurer.
  • Keep records of dates, calls, invoices and readings.
  • Communicate with the tenant about access and timing.

Document everything

Dates and photos matter more than opinions. Record when the water started, who was told and when, and what was damaged. Keep copies of texts and emails. If you are a tenant, keep receipts for temporary lodging if you were displaced. If you are a landlord, keep moisture readings and the written scope.

Moving out, rent and repairs

Whether rent is reduced or the unit is declared uninhabitable depends on the lease and the law. Do not assume. Ask in writing. Mold can follow if drying is slow, so push for fast drying. Read about structural drying and carpet water damage to know what to ask.

When the damage came from a neighbor's unit

In apartments and condos, water often comes from the unit above. Tell the landlord or building manager at once. Photograph the damage, record dates and ask who is contacting the other unit's owner or insurer. Do not enter another unit without permission. The restoration crew can document your side and dry what is wet.

Rent, lodging and returning to the unit

If the unit cannot be used while drying and repair are underway, the lease and state law decide what happens to rent. Ask in writing for the plan and the timeline. Keep receipts for hotels and meals if you are displaced, and check whether your renters policy covers additional living expenses. Do not move back until the crew has recorded dry readings and any needed repairs are complete.

Keeping records as a landlord

For a landlord, a clear paper trail protects both sides. Record when the tenant reported the water, when you responded and who did what. Keep the crew's scope, readings and invoices. Send the tenant a short written update with dates. Good records support insurance, and they reduce disputes about timing or condition.

Both sides can ask the crew whether it follows the ANSI/IICRC S500 water damage standard and request the final drying readings in writing.

Local note: where Florida landlord and tenant rules live

Florida's Residential Landlord and Tenant Act is Part II of Chapter 83 of the Florida Statutes. It is the starting point for questions about repairs and notices, and you can read it on the Florida Legislature's website. Your lease may add more detail. For legal questions, ask a Florida attorney or a local legal aid office.

This applies in Mary Esther and Valparaiso as it does in Fort Walton Beach.

Questions readers ask

Should a tenant call a restoration company directly?

In an emergency, shut off the source and call the landlord. A tenant may also call the dispatch line to get water removal started, then tell the landlord.

Does renters insurance cover water damage?

It often covers your belongings after a sudden event. It usually does not cover the building. Ask your insurer.

Who pays for hotel stays?

It depends on the lease and policy. Keep receipts and ask your insurer.

Can a landlord enter to dry the unit?

Access rules come from the lease and state law. Communicate in writing.

Where can I read Florida's landlord tenant law?

It is in Chapter 83, Part II, of the Florida Statutes, on the Legislature's website.

Can a tenant withhold rent after water damage?

That is a legal question. Do not assume. Read your lease and consult a Florida attorney or a legal aid office.

Who is responsible if mold appears?

Responsibility depends on the cause and the lease. Document the damage and report it promptly, and ask a physician about health concerns.
