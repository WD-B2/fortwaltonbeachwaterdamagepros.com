Emergency Commercial Water Damage in Fort Walton Beach, FL

Water in a business shuts down work and risks inventory. Call the dispatch line for extraction, drying and documentation that supports your claim.

Call (844) 950-6900
24/7 dispatch line open

A flooded storefront, office or restaurant loses money every hour it stays closed. Our dispatch line is open 24/7, and a commercial call starts with a few questions about the building size, the water source and whether utilities are off. Shut off the water source, protect inventory and records, and keep customers and staff out of wet areas.

First steps for a business water loss

  • Close the water source or main and cut power to wet areas if it is safe.
  • Move inventory, files, electronics and records onto shelves or racks.
  • Photograph or video everything before it is moved.
  • Alert your landlord, property manager or building engineer if you lease.
  • Notify your insurer and ask about business interruption coverage.
  • Keep a log of calls, names and times for the claim.
  • If you smell gas, see sparks or hear buzzing near wet wiring, leave and call 911 or the utility before you call us.

What commercial restoration includes

  1. Assess scope and access

    The crew walks the space, checks ceilings and floors across suites, and identifies the source.

  2. Extract and protect

    Pumps and extractors remove water. Contents are lifted or moved to dry areas.

  3. Dry the structure

    High-capacity air movers and dehumidifiers run on a schedule that limits downtime. See structural drying.

  4. Document the loss

    Photos, moisture logs and a written scope support your claim and your landlord.

Phases of a commercial job

  1. Emergency phase

    Stop the water, extract, protect inventory and secure the building. This usually happens the first day.

  2. Drying phase

    High-capacity equipment runs. The crew monitors readings and keeps the area as usable as safely possible.

  3. Rebuild phase

    Ceiling tile, drywall, flooring and finishes are repaired. Tenant improvements follow the lease and the landlord's rules.

What to gather for your landlord and insurer

  • A copy of your lease, including repair and notice terms.
  • Your insurance policy and the agent's contact information.
  • Inventory lists and equipment serial numbers.
  • Photos or video taken before cleanup begins.
  • A log of staff hours and lost sales while the space was closed.
  • Names and times of every call you make about the loss.

Commercial insurers often ask for more documents than a homeowner's insurer does. Starting the file on day one avoids a scramble later.

What affects commercial water damage costs

  • Square footage, number of floors and tenants affected.
  • Water category and source.
  • Ceiling, carpet and wall materials.
  • Contents that must be moved or protected.
  • Drying days and equipment volume.
  • After-hours work to limit downtime.

Restaurants, retail, offices and multi-unit buildings

Restaurants face kitchen drains, grease traps and cooler leaks. Retail faces roof leaks and sprinkler discharge that soak inventory. Offices face supply line breaks above drop ceilings. Multi-unit buildings need coordination between tenants and management. Fort Walton Beach sits near the Gulf, so storm-driven water is a recurring concern. Insurance note for commercial claims

Commercial policies often include property damage and business interruption, with limits and waiting periods that vary. Read your policy or ask your agent, and keep records of lost income.

Planning ahead for a business water loss

  • Keep a copy of your insurance policy and agent's phone number off-site.
  • Label shutoff valves and share locations with staff.
  • Photograph inventory and equipment once a year.
  • Keep key documents on shelves, not on the floor.
  • Ask your landlord who handles building water losses.

Commercial drying plans commonly reference the ANSI/IICRC S500 standard, and the same document is a good reference when you ask a contractor how they will measure and report progress.

Questions we hear from Fort Walton Beach callers

Do you handle commercial water damage emergencies at night or on weekends in Fort Walton Beach?

Yes. The dispatch line is open 24/7. A business loss on a weekend is common, and the dispatcher helps you plan the first steps.

Can you work around business hours?

We can discuss scheduling to limit downtime. Some work, such as extraction, cannot wait.

Do you help with documentation for my insurer?

Yes. Photos, moisture logs and a written scope support your claim. We do not promise approval.

What if I rent the space?

Your lease decides who handles repair. Tell your landlord right away and follow the lease notice terms.

Can inventory be saved?

Sometimes. Move it to a dry area quickly. Items that touched contaminated water are usually discarded.

Can you work in occupied buildings?

Often yes, with containment and scheduling. Tell the dispatcher about tenants and hours.
