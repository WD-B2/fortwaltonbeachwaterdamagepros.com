Ice inside a pipe can crack the wall of the pipe, and the leak often begins only when the ice thaws. Our dispatch line is open 24/7, so call as soon as you see water, a stain or a drop in pressure. Shut the main valve first and keep the heat on while you wait.

Why warm-climate homes have frozen pipe problems

Homes in warm regions are often built with supply lines in attics, exterior walls and outdoor spigots that have little insulation. When a cold snap does arrive, those exposed lines are the first to freeze. Water expands as it turns to ice, and that pressure splits fittings and thin pipe walls.

Because the freeze may happen overnight and the split shows up at thaw time, the first sign is often a ceiling stain, a wet wall or a pooled floor in the morning.

What to do if you think a pipe froze

  • Close the main shutoff valve if you see any water, then open a faucet to relieve pressure.
  • Keep the thermostat on and open cabinet doors under sinks on exterior walls so warm air reaches the pipes.
  • Thaw only with gentle warmth, such as a hair dryer from the faucet end. Never use a torch or open flame.
  • If a pipe is split or a ceiling is wet, stay out from under bulging drywall and avoid outlets in that area.
  • Photograph frost, splits and wet surfaces for your records.
  • Call a licensed plumber for repair and the restoration line for water removal.
  • If you smell gas, see sparks or hear buzzing near wet wiring, leave and call 911 or the utility before you call us.

After the pipe thaws: how cleanup proceeds

  1. Locate every wet area

    Frozen lines often run through several spaces. The crew traces the line with a meter and a thermal camera instead of guessing.

  2. Remove standing water and wet insulation

    Attic and wall insulation holds water against drywall. It is removed when it cannot dry in place.

  3. Dry cavities and ceilings

    Air movers and dehumidifiers run until readings reach your dry standard. See structural drying for how drying goals are set.

  4. Hand off for rebuild

    Dried structure is ready for drywall, paint or insulation repair, which are separate from the cleanup scope.

What affects the cost of frozen pipe cleanup

  • Number of splits and the amount of water released.
  • Whether the leak ran inside an attic, wall or ceiling, which hides water.
  • Insulation and drywall that must be removed and replaced.
  • Time between the split and the shutoff.
  • Equipment days needed to reach dry readings.
  • Rebuild items, which are quoted separately from water removal.

Prevent the next freeze

Insulate exposed lines in attics and exterior walls. Disconnect garden hoses and cover outdoor spigots before a forecast freeze. Learn where your main valve is, and test that it turns. A plumber can add foam sleeves or reroute a vulnerable line. Preventing a freeze costs far less than cleaning up behind a split pipe.

If the failure was in a supply line rather than from ice, read about burst pipe water damage. If the leak is a slow drip in a ceiling, see ceiling water damage repair.

Insurance note for frozen pipes

Policies often cover sudden pipe freezing damage when the home was kept heated, and they may exclude damage when the house was vacant or the heat was off. Check your policy language or ask your insurer, and keep photos of the split and the repair invoice.

Questions we hear from Fort Walton Beach callers

Do you handle frozen pipe emergencies at night or on weekends in Fort Walton Beach?

Yes. The 24/7 line takes calls any hour. Freeze-related leaks often show up at thaw time, which can be early morning or a weekend, so the dispatcher can send a crew for extraction and drying whenever you call.

Can a pipe freeze in Florida?

Yes. Any exposed or poorly insulated line can freeze when temperatures fall below freezing for long enough. Lines in attics, exterior walls and outdoor spigots are the usual weak points.

Should I thaw a frozen pipe myself?

Only with gentle heat such as a hair dryer, and only if you see no leaks. Never use an open flame. If the pipe is split, close the main valve and call a plumber.

Does a frozen pipe always burst?

No. Some lines freeze and thaw without splitting. The risk is higher with older pipe, tight fittings and rapid thaw. Look for stains and moisture after the thaw.

How do I know if water is hiding inside the wall?

A moisture meter and thermal camera find it. Signs you can see include bubbling paint, a musty smell, warped baseboards and stains that grow.
